# Feasibility Assessment and Evidence Requirements

This Reference owns decision criteria and required evidence, not generic source investigation. Use already sufficient evidence. A missing material fact becomes a bounded research question under the installed `research` method; no automatic research or delegation preflight.

- Assess options against evidence for existing code, standard-library capabilities and maintained open source. Retain packages, callable interfaces, versions, dates and sources in the decision record. Evidence must resolve applicable archive/deprecation/sunset status, replacements, releases/changelogs and maintainer responsiveness relative to maturity. Popularity/familiarity proves no maintenance; mark unverified options pending, excluding long-term recommendations.
- Compare minimal sufficient candidates: existing capabilities, open source with thin adaptation, and demonstrated custom gaps. Explain inapplicable options. Compare example coverage/gaps, licensing, compatibility, installation/transitive dependencies, resources, tokens/actual costs, upgrades, testing, migration, and maintenance. Keep frameworks provisional.
- Define hypotheses, real inputs, expected results, criteria, and boundaries before isolated end-to-end experiments. Verify content/relationships, normal/boundary/negative cases, local compatibility, resources/costs, and reuse gaps. Experimental code stays outside application pipelines; installation/downloads/calls/runs remain within authorized research.
- Show example/requirement → function/interface → actual evidence → gap/failure. Distinguish documented support, inspected source, local execution, feasibility conclusions, and user acceptance. Mark unmeasured capabilities unverified; explain blocked experiments and next steps.
- Finalize frameworks only when agreed critical paths meet confirmed criteria, limitations and failed/unverified items are evidenced, and the user confirms findings/tradeoffs. Otherwise continue research or explicitly reconfirm scope changes.
